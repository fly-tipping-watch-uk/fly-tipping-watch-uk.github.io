Editorial and evidence standards

Credibility is the main source of pressure

Every public statement should be traceable, proportionate, correctable and clearly attributed.

Facts before conclusions

Distinguish direct observation, documentary evidence, a public-body statement and reasonable inference.

No public identification of suspected offenders

Do not publish names, faces, registration plates or allegations concerning private individuals. Supply relevant material to the investigating authority.

Personal-data minimisation

Redact private email addresses, telephone numbers, signatures and unrelated residential details.

Original evidence retained privately

Publish only necessary copies and keep a note of resizing, cropping or redaction.

Right of reply

Invite relevant public bodies to correct factual errors and provide an attributable response. Display substantive replies fairly.

Visible correction history

Correct mistakes promptly and record material changes to published cases.

Evidence-based status labels

Use “overdue” only when a published target or case-specific promised date has passed.

Outcomes updated prominently

When action is completed, record it clearly. The purpose is action and accountability, not permanent criticism.
